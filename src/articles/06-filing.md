The USCIS page for first preference (EB-1) covers three different groups. People often mix them up, so here is what the official page says about each.

## Three categories under EB-1

- **Extraordinary ability (often called EB-1A).** You show sustained national or international acclaim. No offer of employment and no labor certification is required.
- **Outstanding professors and researchers (EB-1B).** You show international recognition in an academic field, at least three years of teaching or research experience, and a job offer from a US employer. You must meet at least two of six listed criteria.
- **Multinational managers and executives (EB-1C).** For certain managers and executives transferring to a related US company.

## Who files

For extraordinary ability, **you may apply for yourself** by filing Form I-140, Petition for Alien Worker.

For the other two categories, your US employer must file the Form I-140 and show it can pay the offered wage.

## Your family

USCIS states that if your I-140 petition is approved, your spouse and unmarried children under 21 may be eligible to apply for admission to the United States in E-14 or E-15 immigrant status.

## Fees and timing

Fees and processing times change. We do not quote them here because a number that is right today may be wrong next month. Check these official sources on the day you plan to file:

- The USCIS fee information for Form I-140
- The USCIS processing times tool
- The Department of State Visa Bulletin, which shows whether a visa number is available for your category and country

## Before you file

An approved petition depends on the evidence submitted with it. USCIS decides on what is in the file. Organise your evidence under each criterion you claim, and make every exhibit easy to verify.
