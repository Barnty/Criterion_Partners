A Request for Evidence (RFE) means the officer is not yet convinced and is giving you a chance to fill the gaps. An RFE is not a denial, but it costs time and stress. Most RFEs come from a few recurring weaknesses, and nearly all of them can be prevented.

## 1. Evidence that meets the words but not the standard

A petition can technically fit a criterion and still fall short. A "membership" that only requires a fee, or "media coverage" that is really a press release, may be questioned because it does not show the achievement the criterion is meant to capture.

**Prevent it:** for each criterion, include proof of why the item is meaningful, such as membership rules, selection rates, or the reach of a publication.

## 2. Significance asserted, not shown

"Original contributions of major significance" is where many cases struggle. Saying your work is important is not the same as showing others in the field rely on it.

**Prevent it:** show use and impact from outside your own group: citations, adoption, licences, independent letters explaining what changed because of your work.

## 3. Letters that only praise

Letters full of superlatives without specifics, or written only by close colleagues, often prompt questions about whether recognition reaches beyond your circle.

**Prevent it:** include independent recommenders and concrete examples. Our guide to [recommendation letters](eb1a-recommendation-letters.html) covers this in detail.

## 4. Missing context

An officer is not an expert in your field. If the file does not explain why a journal, award or role is prestigious, the officer cannot assume it.

**Prevent it:** add short, sourced context for each key item: who runs it, how selective it is, how it is regarded.

## 5. Recognition that is not sustained

The standard is sustained acclaim. A single burst of activity years ago, with little since, can raise doubts.

**Prevent it:** show a continuing record, including recent work, reviews and recognition.

## 6. The final merits question

Even when three criteria are met, the officer weighs the whole record to decide whether you are among the small percentage at the top of your field. A collection of thin items can fail this step.

**Prevent it:** build a connected story where your publications, judging work and recognition point to the same expertise. Read more in [what the USCIS EB-1 page says about the final merits step](what-uscis-says-about-the-final-merits-step.html).
