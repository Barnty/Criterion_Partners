Recommendation letters do not meet any EB-1A criterion on their own. What they do is explain your evidence: why a contribution matters, how your work is used, and how you compare with others in the field. A strong letter makes the rest of the record easier to understand. A weak one adds pages and nothing else.

## Who should write them

Aim for a mix:

- **People who know your work directly**, such as a supervisor or collaborator. They can describe exactly what you did.
- **Independent experts** who have never worked with you but know your work through your publications, talks or tools. These letters show your reputation has travelled beyond your own circle, which is the point of the category.

A petition supported only by colleagues from one institution can make the acclaim look local. Independent voices answer that question before it is asked.

## What makes a letter strong

- **Specifics, not praise.** "One of the most talented researchers I have met" says little. "Her method cut processing time by 40 percent and is now used by three groups I know of" says a great deal.
- **Tied to criteria.** Each letter should support particular claims in the petition, for example your original contributions or the significance of a role.
- **The writer's own standing.** A short description of the recommender's credentials explains why their opinion carries weight.
- **How they know your work.** Especially for independent writers, say how they came across it.
- **Consistent with the evidence.** Every claim in a letter should be backed by something else in the file.

## What to avoid

- Letters that read alike, which suggests one author
- Claims no other document supports
- Vague superlatives with no comparison or example
- Too many letters saying the same thing. A few strong letters beat many thin ones.

## How to ask

Ask early, explain what the petition is for, and offer a short summary of the work you would like them to address. The words and the opinion must be theirs. A recommender should only sign a letter they genuinely agree with.
