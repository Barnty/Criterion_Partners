Read the official USCIS page on EB-1 and one phrase appears before any list of criteria: you must demonstrate extraordinary ability "through sustained national or international acclaim."

The ten criteria come second. They are how you show the acclaim. They are not the definition of it.

## What the page actually says

USCIS states that to demonstrate sustained acclaim, and that your achievements have been recognised in your field, you must include either evidence of a one-time achievement (a major internationally recognised award) or evidence under three of the ten listed criteria, or comparable evidence if a criterion does not readily apply.

The page then points readers to the USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 6, Part F, Chapter 2, for the full policy.

## Why that matters

The Policy Manual describes a two-part review. First, the officer checks whether the evidence meets at least three criteria. Second, the officer weighs all the evidence together to decide whether the person has sustained acclaim and is among the small percentage at the top of the field.

So a petition can meet three criteria and still be denied at the second part.

## What this means for your evidence

- **Each item should stand on its own.** Could a stranger verify it without calling you?
- **The items should connect.** Reviews, publications, citations and recognition that point to the same expertise tell one story.
- **Recent activity counts.** "Sustained" means the recognition did not stop years ago.

## Our view

We build profiles for the second part, not the first. Three ticks are easy to manufacture, and that is exactly why officers look past them.
