EB-1A or EB-2 NIW: which fits your record?

Two self-petition routes, two different standards. How to tell which is yours.

Both EB-1A and EB-2 with a National Interest Waiver (NIW) let you petition for yourself. Neither needs an employer to sponsor you. That is where the similarity ends.

EB-1A: about you

EB-1A asks whether you are extraordinary. You show sustained national or international acclaim, usually through at least three of the ten criteria, and the officer then weighs the whole record.

  • No job offer needed
  • No labor certification needed
  • You file Form I-140 yourself
  • The standard is high: the top of your field

EB-2 NIW: about your work

NIW asks whether your proposed work matters to the United States. You first qualify for EB-2 (an advanced degree, or exceptional ability). Then you ask USCIS to waive the usual job offer requirement because your work is in the national interest.

Under the framework USCIS uses, you show three things:

  1. Your proposed work has substantial merit and national importance.
  2. You are well positioned to advance it.
  3. On balance, it benefits the United States to waive the job offer requirement.

How to tell which fits

EB-1A may fit if you already have recognition others can verify: peer review invitations, selective memberships, awards, press, a strong citation record.

NIW may fit if your work is clearly important and you have a solid track record, but the outside recognition is still growing.

Many people qualify for NIW today and for EB-1A after another year or two of deliberate profile building. Some file both.

One practical point

How long you wait for a green card depends on your category and your country of birth. EB-1 and EB-2 can have very different waiting times. Check the current Visa Bulletin from the US Department of State before choosing.

This article is general information, not legal advice. Which category to file in is a legal decision. Speak to a licensed immigration attorney about your own case.
