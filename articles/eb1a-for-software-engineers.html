EB-1A for software engineers: which criteria actually fit

The criteria that usually fit engineers, the evidence that works, and the traps to avoid.

Software engineers increasingly ask whether EB-1A is realistic for them. It can be, but the criteria were written broadly, and engineers often reach for the wrong ones. Here is where engineering work tends to fit, and where it tends to fall short.

The criteria that most often fit

Original contributions of major significance. This is usually the centre of an engineer's case. The key word is *significance*: not that you built something, but that it changed how others work. Evidence that tends to help:

  • Wide adoption of software or tools you created, shown with numbers others can verify
  • Open-source projects that other companies or projects depend on
  • Patents that are licensed or built into products
  • Independent experts explaining what your work changed in the field

High salary. USCIS compares your pay with others in your field. Total compensation can matter, and published salary data for your role and location gives the comparison. Your own pay slips alone do not show it is high.

Leading or critical role. A role that was critical to a distinguished organisation, shown through what you led and why it mattered, not just your job title.

Judging. Reviewing for technical conferences or journals, or serving as a judge in recognised technical competitions.

Scholarly articles. Papers at respected conferences or journals. Engineering blog posts usually do not meet this criterion on their own.

Where engineers commonly go wrong

  • Leaning on job titles. "Senior" or "Staff" describes your level inside one company. It does not show acclaim in the field.
  • Internal praise. Letters and awards from inside your own employer show your company values you. The criteria look for recognition beyond your immediate circle.
  • Counting features shipped. Building a product feature is your job. Showing it had major significance for others is the case.
  • Unverifiable numbers. Claims like "used by millions" need independent support an officer can check.

A practical way to start

Write down your three strongest pieces of work. For each, ask: who outside my company uses this, cites it, or would say it matters? Where the honest answer is "nobody yet", that is where profile building starts, for example writing up the work as a paper, contributing it publicly, or presenting it at a conference.

Three criteria get you considered. A record showing the field recognises your work is what gets you approved. Our guide to the ten EB-1A criteria explains the difference.
