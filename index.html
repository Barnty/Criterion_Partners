The actual test

Nobody at USCIS is counting your boxes.

The regulation lists ten criteria. Meeting three of them makes you eligible to be considered. It does not make you approved. The officer then steps back and asks one question: has this person risen to the top of their field? Most petitions fail right there, holding a full checklist.

The 2026 problem

Approvals are being taken back.

USCIS is reopening petitions it already approved. The notices name the same four things every time: papers published in journals that accept anything within days, citations traded between accounts, awards that arrive once the entry fee clears, and press releases written by a consultant and placed on sites that publish anything. A lot of firms sell exactly those four things. We do not, and we will tell you no if you ask.

We do not

Pay-to-publish journals

If a journal accepts your paper in 48 hours, it will not survive a second look. We target journals with real editorial boards and real rejection rates.

We do not

Traded citations

A citation ring is the easiest pattern in the world to spot from the outside. We build citations the slow way, through work people actually find and use.

We do not

Awards you can buy

If the entry fee is the only barrier, an officer will find that out. We go after awards with a selection process and a public record of who has won before.

We do not

Placed press

A release on a site that publishes anything is not media coverage. We work toward coverage a journalist chose to write.
